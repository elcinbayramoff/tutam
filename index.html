Tutam

Local-first personal finance tracker for Android

Logging an expense should take one tap, not a form. Tutam is built around templates for the things you buy every day — and everything stays on your phone.

Why it's different

  • One tap to log. Tap “Coffee”, done. From the app or the home-screen widget.
  • Or no taps at all. Pay with Google Wallet and Tutam can read the amount and merchant from the payment notification, then wait on your home screen for you to confirm it.
  • No account, ever. Nothing to sign up for. Start logging the second it opens.
  • No network permission. The release build does not request INTERNET, so your records cannot leave the device.
  • Yours to take. Full backups (optionally encrypted), CSV export, and automatic encrypted snapshots to a folder you pick.
  • Four languages. English, Azərbaycanca, Türkçe, Русский.

What's inside

  • Monthly targets with a pace indicator, and per-category budgets with live progress
  • Spending by category, income vs expense, an activity heatmap, and every period compared against the one before
  • Reminders that arrive as notifications with Log and Snooze buttons
  • Recurring entries for rent, salary and subscriptions
  • A searchable history with undo, and a cash-or-card tag on every entry
  • Track what you lent and what you borrowed, and settle it later
  • Optional biometric lock

Automatic capture, and what it reads

Capture is off until you turn it on. Tutam explains what it will read before Android's notification access screen appears, and it reads notifications from the Google Wallet app only — enforced in code, not merely promised. The notification text is erased as soon as you confirm or dismiss the entry. Tutam has no internet permission, so none of it can leave your phone. The privacy policy covers this in full.
